Case study, analytics and growth

Ads got 44% cheaper. Should we spend more?

A B2B food brand ran a four-week search-ads pilot. I tuned bids and search terms until cost per click fell 44%. The dashboard said scale; before touching the budget, I measured what the clicks actually did.

01

What the dashboard said.

Cost per click fell every week, 44% by week four, and the default next move was a larger budget. A cheaper click is only good news if it converts, and the dashboard did not measure that.

02

What those visitors did.

I joined every ad click ID and UTM parameter to site sessions in SQL, then measured the share of sessions that took a next step.

Session
One visit. Bots removed by user agent; internal traffic removed by IP.
Ad visitor
A session whose first page view carries an ad click ID or a cpc tag.
Next step
A contact, wholesale, product or sample click, or opening the wholesale form.
2.7%of ad visitors took a next step (95% CI 1.6 to 4.5)
17%of all other visitors (95% CI 11.2 to 24.1)

CorrectionThe first count included cookie-banner “Got it” clicks and put other visitors at 19%. Excluding them changed the estimates, not the decision.

03

A fairer comparison.

All ad traffic landed on the wholesale page, while most other visitors entered through the homepage and many already knew the brand. Restricting to wholesale-page landings controls for part of that selection.

2.7%of ad visitors
7.3%of other visitors on the wholesale page (95% CI 2.5 to 19.4)

The gap narrows, but the ad rate remains less than half.

What this does not claimThis is an observational comparison, not the causal effect of the ads. A causal read needs a geo or time-based holdout; the pilot’s budget did not justify one.

04

Why the clicks were cheap.

Two broad-match terms drove 71% of ad clicks, from buyers looking for a general distributor rather than a specialty manufacturer. Product-name searches, the buyers actually looking for us, were 7% of clicks.

Net: roughly 37 ad clicks per next step.

DisclosureSearch terms are anonymised as A and B; the keyword research belongs to the business.

Decision

Pause the broad ads.

A lower cost per click does not rescue a conversion rate this low, and unpaid channels were already converting better. Any future paid test would be limited to product-name terms and read against a holdout.

Next

Channel or visitor? Mostly where they came from.

To find what predicts a next step on the homepage, I trained a gradient-boosted classifier on landing context and the first 30 seconds of behaviour.

0.69ROC AUC, traffic source only
0.69ROC AUC, all features

Additional features added no lift over traffic source. Organic-search visitors converted at 26.9% and referral visitors at 6.5%, about 4× apart, which pointed acquisition toward organic search.

Leakage removed before training

  • Largest Contentful Paint, which the browser reports after interaction and so encodes the outcome.
  • Sessions landing on the wholesale page, which cannot click the homepage call to action.
  • Any behaviour after the first click; early signals are truncated before it, at 30 seconds.

CorrectionWith cookie-banner clicks in the label, the models scored 0.75 and 0.77. Corrected, both are 0.69; the conclusion held.

Result

Earn the top result instead.

With paid search paused, I rebuilt the site for generative engine optimization (GEO): to be read, trusted and cited by AI answer engines, not only ranked by Google.

  • An llms.txt brief for AI crawlers: who the company is, what it makes, and a map of the site.
  • Schema.org structured data for the organization, every product, and a 24-question FAQ.
  • A reference page that defines the product category and covers every name buyers search for it under, across languages.
  • Local press coverage linked as third-party corroboration.
#1for the core category search on Google, Gemini and ChatGPT

Also

The A/B test I sized, and did not launch.

Before redesigning the homepage, I sized the test: two-sided, α = 0.05, power 0.80, minimum detectable lift 30%.

2,458sessions required on the corrected 13.7% baseline
17.8%conversion the variant would need to reach

On the original 14.2% baseline the requirement was 2,358. Either way it exceeded what the site could deliver in a reasonable window, so I documented the design rather than run a test that could only end inconclusive.
