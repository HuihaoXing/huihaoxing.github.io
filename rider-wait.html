Case study, metric design

A newsroom has 20 million boardings. Which riders are being failed?

GBH News wanted to know which MBTA bus riders were being failed. With a BU Spark! team, I built a measure of the wait riders actually feel, and the newsroom adopted it.

Role
Data science research analyst
Partner
GBH News, through BU Spark!
Stack
Python, pandas, Tableau
Data
MBTA arrivals and departures for 2019 and 2022, ridership by trip, the 2023 passenger survey

01

What the schedule says.

The standard measure compares each bus with its own timetable, and by that measure most routes look on time. It breaks down when buses bunch or pass each other, and it counts a bus that leaves a minute early as on time, even though a rider who arrived on schedule just missed it.

02

What riders feel.

So I measured from the rider’s side. A rider arrives at the scheduled time and boards the next bus that actually leaves; the wait is the gap between the two. Leaving within 60 seconds of schedule counts as on time, and a bus that leaves early is a missed bus.

03

Route 14.

Route 14 stood out in 2019 and again in 2022. It runs so few trips that one missed bus is expensive: miss the 1:31 pm and the next one leaves at 3:21 pm.

16+ minaverage wait on Route 14, in both years

Read with careThe average is driven by a few very long gaps, so a median or percentile view belongs beside it.

04

Where the waits fall.

Joining route delays to rider demographics, the longest waits clustered on routes through Environmental Justice neighborhoods such as Roxbury and Dorchester.

A limitThe passenger survey covered only some routes, so the demographic comparison rests on routes 15, 22, 23, 28, 29 and 111.

Decision

Route 14 riders wait 16+ minutes.

The project team and a senior GBH editor adopted the rider wait as their main measure of service reliability, and I walked reporters through the findings each week in Tableau.

Behind it

Data, cleaning and limits.

  • MBTA bus arrival and departure times for 2019 and 2022, one year before the pandemic and one after.
  • Ridership by trip, season, route and stop, fall 2016 to fall 2022.
  • The 2023 system-wide passenger survey, for rider demographics by route.
  • Missing trip, time-point and actual-time fields affected under 5% of rows and were handled before any comparison.
  • Buses that left out of order were matched to riders by actual departure, which is the point of the measure.

The limit I would flag firstThe measure assumes riders arrive at the scheduled time. That fits infrequent routes; on frequent routes riders arrive at random, and a headway-based measure is better.
