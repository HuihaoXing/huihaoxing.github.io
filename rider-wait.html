Rider wait-time metric · GBH News, with BU Spark!

On the timetable, Boston’s buses look on time. So why do some riders wait so long?

In 30 seconds

Context
GBH News asked which Boston bus riders were being failed. The standard on-time measure made most routes look reliable.
What I did
With a BU Spark! team, built a measure of how long riders actually wait from 5+ years of schedule, GPS and ridership data in Python, and walked reporters through it weekly in Tableau.
Result
The GBH project team and a senior editor adopted it as their primary reliability measure. Route 14 riders averaged 16+ minutes.
Limits
Assumes riders arrive at scheduled times, which suits low-frequency routes, not frequent ones.

01

What the schedule says.

The standard on-time measure compares each bus with its own timetable, and by that measure most routes look reliable. On the right, one line per route and one dot per bus; Route 14 is in blue. It breaks down when buses bunch or overtake one another, and it scores an early departure as on time, even though a rider who arrived on schedule has just missed it.

02

What riders experience.

I measured from the rider’s side: a rider arrives at the scheduled time and boards the next bus that actually departs; the wait is the difference. Departures within 60 seconds of schedule count as on time, and an early departure counts as a missed bus.

About the sceneTicks on the right mark the timetable; dots are the buses as they ran. The bunched departures are illustrative, not observed data.

04

Across all 15 routes.

Ranked by average rider wait, Route 14 stood out in both 2019 and 2022. Joining route-level waits to rider demographics, the longest waits concentrated on routes serving Environmental Justice neighborhoods such as Roxbury and Dorchester.

16+ minaverage rider wait on Route 14, in both years

CaveatThe mean is driven by a few very long gaps; a median or percentile view belongs alongside it. Bar lengths in the scene are illustrative.

CoverageThe passenger survey covers only some routes, so the demographic comparison rests on routes 15, 22, 23, 28, 29 and 111.

Outcome

GBH’s project team now measures reliability by rider wait.

The project team and a senior GBH editor adopted rider wait as their primary measure of service reliability, and I briefed reporters on the findings weekly in Tableau.

Method

Data, cleaning and limits.

  • MBTA bus arrival and departure times for 2019 and 2022, one year before the pandemic and one after.
  • Ridership by trip, season, route and stop, fall 2016 to fall 2022.
  • The 2023 system-wide passenger survey, for rider demographics by route.
  • Missing trip, time-point and actual-time fields affected under 5% of rows and were resolved before any comparison.
  • Buses departing out of order were matched to riders by actual departure, which is the premise of the measure.

Primary limitationThe measure assumes riders arrive at the scheduled time, which suits low-frequency routes. On high-frequency routes arrivals are closer to random, and a headway-based measure is more appropriate.
