Case study, AI systems

Each lead took 15 minutes of research. Can AI help without going rogue?

A B2B food brand preparing to launch needed importers and specialty distributors, with one person owning everything technical. I designed, built and ran the prospecting system that finds them.

Role
Sole designer and engineer
Stack
TypeScript, Next.js, Claude and OpenAI APIs, MCP, HubSpot
Status
In use before launch

First

The naive version fails twice.

Cost inversion. Deep research is the expensive step, and running it on every scraped candidate pays the most for the leads you know least about.

Trust. Auto-sent AI outreach reads as spam, and in food import, a relationship business, one bad first touch closes a door.

So the design question was never how much the AI could do. It was what it must never do.

01

Find candidates.

Deterministic search against business-directory APIs, deduplicated against everything already seen. No model calls, so it stays cheap by construction.

02

A person decides.

A person reviews each candidate and keeps or passes it. Research spend is unlocked only by that decision, so the human is the budget allocator, not a rubber stamp at the end.

03

Research with sources.

Enrichment runs on kept candidates only. Every claim carries a public citation, and anything that cannot be traced stays blank. A model then gives verdicts a reviewer can challenge: buyer or competitor, product fit, and why.

Blank beats plausibleAn honest gap costs a follow-up question. A confident hallucination in a first email costs the relationship.

04

A draft, never sent.

The last step writes a review-ready letter and its evidence dossier. Sending opens the person’s own mail; the app holds no mail credentials. The capability does not exist in the code, which is what makes every other step safe to automate.

Result

2 to 5 minutes per lead, not 15.

2–5 minhuman review per lead, down from about 15
$0.06–0.12model cost per researched lead
0emails the system can send on its own

Cheap is not the point by itself; bounded is. Search ceilings and budget caps mean the worst-case bill is known before a run starts.

Under it

Tools, not a script.

The pipeline is an MCP server, so each stage is a tool an agent or a person can call, with cost limits and the gate enforced in code rather than by prompt. Each step uses the cheapest Claude or OpenAI model that clears the bar, behind strict TypeScript and 300+ automated tests.

Not yet evaluated at scaleThe brand has not launched, so only the first dozen or so drafts have been reviewed, too few for a formal evaluation. That is why the design leans on a human gate and hard cost ceilings.
