Case study · 01 / SALES_AI

Spend only after signal.

A prospecting system I designed, built, and ran solo for a real B2B food business. It finds candidate buyers cheaply, lets a human decide who deserves research, backs every claim with a public source, and drafts outreach that it is structurally unable to send.

Solo buildClaude + OpenAIMCP serverHuman-gated2026

The problem

Soja Foods is a U.S. tofu-skin producer preparing for launch. It needed a pipeline of importers and specialty distributors — classic B2B prospecting — with one person (me) owning everything technical.

The naive LLM version of this fails in two predictable ways. First, cost inversion: deep research is the expensive step, and running it on every scraped candidate means paying the most for the leads you know the least about. Second, trust destruction: auto-sent AI outreach reads as spam, and in food import — a relationship business — one bad first touch closes a door permanently.

So the design question was never "how much can the AI do?" It was "what must the AI never do?"

The architecture

Five stages · one human gate · nothing sends automatically

01 · DiscoverDeterministic search against business directory APIs. Dedup against everything already seen. Cheap by construction — no LLM calls yet.
Human gate02 · Keep / passA person reviews each candidate and decides who is worth money. Research spend is only unlocked by this decision.
03 · ResearchSource-backed enrichment on kept candidates only. Every claim carries a public citation; unverifiable fields stay blank.
04 · JudgeStructured verdicts, not vibes: buyer vs. competitor, product-spec fit, and why — recorded as explicit fields a reviewer can challenge.
05 · DraftA review-ready letter plus the evidence dossier, handed to a person. The system has no send capability at all.

What it cost

$0.59Full acceptance run, discovery through reviewed draft
1Human decision required before any research spend
0Emails the system can send on its own

Cheap is not the point by itself — bounded is the point. Search ceilings and budget caps mean the worst-case bill is known before a run starts, which is what makes it safe to hand to a non-technical operator.

Decisions I'd defend

Deterministic before generative

Discovery and dedup are plain code. LLMs enter only where judgment is genuinely required — reading sources, assessing fit, writing. This is why the cost curve stays flat as the candidate pool grows.

The gate is upstream of the spend

Most "human in the loop" designs put the human at the end, reviewing output the system already paid to produce. Putting the keep/pass decision before enrichment makes human judgment the budget allocator, not a rubber stamp.

Blank beats plausible

If a fact can't be traced to a public source, the field stays empty. An honest gap costs a follow-up question; a confident hallucination in a first-touch email costs the relationship.

No send path, by construction

Not a config flag — the capability doesn't exist. The deliverable is a draft plus dossier for a person to act on. This single constraint is what makes every other part of the system safe to automate aggressively.

Built as tools, not a script

The system is exposed as an MCP server — each stage is a tool an agent (or a person) can call, with the cost limits and the gate enforced at the tool layer rather than by prompt discipline. Model-side it runs on Claude and OpenAI models, chosen per step for the cheapest model that clears the quality bar.
